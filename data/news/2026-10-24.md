The S&P 500 ( ^GSPC -0.52% ) and Nasdaq Composite ( ^IXIC -0.21% ) have recently been experiencing a bout of volatility after repeatedly notching record highs throughout 2026. With stocks coming under pressure, some investors are wondering whether a larger market correction is approaching.
Part of the concern stems from elevated bond yields and stubborn inflation. Meanwhile, artificial intelligence (AI) hyperscalers like Amazon , Microsoft , Alphabet , and Meta Platforms are among the companies supporting the broader economy as they accelerate spending on data centers .
Whether this volatility is just a temporary pullback or the beginning of something worse is impossible to know. But legendary investor Warren Buffett spent decades explaining how to navigate markets dominated by exciting new technologies, sky-high valuations, and uncertainty.
Former Berkshire Hathaway CEO Warren Buffett. Image source: Getty Images.
Revolutionary technology does not equal a good investment
Buffett built Berkshire Hathaway  into one of history&#x27;s most successful investment firms simply because he had a relentless focus on businesses he understands. He generally avoided technology stocks, instead favoring industries such as insurance, banking, consumer products, utilities, and energy. These businesses usually have relatively predictable economics, which allowed Buffett to better assess their long-term earnings potential.
Buffett understood that an industry can revolutionize society without producing attractive returns overall. Consider the automobile and aviation industries as examples. Cars and airplanes have unquestionably transformed the global economy, but countless early manufacturers and airlines have gone bankrupt as competition intensified.
The theme here is that identifying which industries are game changers is much easier than choosing the specific companies that will capture profits in the long run. This distinction matters in today&#x27;s AI environment.
While AI is shaping up to be one of the most transformative technologies in history, that doesn&#x27;t mean every semiconductor company, software developer, or infrastructure provider is going to generate market-beating returns.
Is the AI revolution destined to end up like the dot-com bubble?
During the late 1990s, investors were pouring money into virtually anything that resembled the internet. Speculative technology stocks became multibaggers , even when the underlying companies had little revenue, no profits, or incomplete business models.
Reality inevitably caught up with expectations. After peaking in March 2000, the Nasdaq plummeted by 78% by October 2002. Suddenly, once-promising internet businesses left investors holding the bag.
Today, some investors are worried the AI boom could become a modern retelling of the dot-com bubble. Sure, AI leaders like Nvidia and the cloud hyperscalers already generate substantial profits and cash flow. The reason why is that their AI infrastructure is supported by numerous established businesses rather than simply promises around future growth. Still, the industry faces uncertainty because not every model developer is going to succeed in the long run.
This brings us to Buffett&#x27;s most important investing principle. The single best thing an investor can do for their portfolio right now is load up on companies with durable competitive advantages rather than chasing the stocks attracting the most attention. Buffett describes these advantages as economic moats that come in the form of powerful brands, high switching costs, network effects , and economies of scale.
There&#x27;s a more subtle lesson investors can learn from the dot-com crash: Time in the market is more valuable than trying to time the market. Suppose you invested in the S&P 500 near its peak during the dot-com era. If you managed to stay invested, you would have witnessed the index appreciate roughly 450%.
A hypothetical $10,000 investment would have grown to nearly $55,000, even though it was made right before a major market collapse. What&#x27;s even more inspiring is that over the last 25 years, the S&P 500 has endured three recessions -- pictured above as the grey-shaded columns -- and managed to come out higher after each one. This underscores the resiliency of the S&P 500 as one of the greatest wealth-creation vehicles.
This performance is why Buffett&#x27;s emphasis on diversification is both valuable and relevant in today&#x27;s market. Rather than panic-selling whenever stocks become volatile or valuation is frothy, investors should rebalance their portfolios if their positions expose them to unnecessary risk.
Ultimately, no one knows whether the recent sell-off will permeate into a full-blown crash. But Buffett&#x27;s philosophy offers a practical response: The best opportunity is to own quality businesses, avoid paying unreasonable prices, and remain patient enough to let compounding do the heavy lifting.

Trump signed a presidential memorandum directing Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to use Section 303 of the Defense Production Act of 1950 to strengthen domestic petroleum production, refining, pipelines and fuel storage facilities. The order identifies those operations as essential to national defense and authorizes federal purchases, financial commitments and agreements to expand industrial capacity.
The directive comes as global energy disruptions linked to the conflict involving Iran and attacks on Russian energy infrastructure continue to pressure fuel markets. As of October 23, the national average price of gasoline stood at approximately $4.29 per gallon, while diesel averaged $6.32, increasing transportation and operating costs for consumers and businesses.
Trump determined that financing constraints, construction timelines, permitting delays and supply chain limitations have prevented private industry from expanding petroleum infrastructure quickly enough to meet national defense needs. The president also waived six statutory requirements governing federal support for industrial expansion, citing the national energy emergency he declared in January 2025. Section 303 of the Defense Production Act allows the government to support industries essential to national defense through purchases, financial commitments and investments in production capacity. Enacted during the Korean War, the statute permits specified requirements to be waived during a declared national emergency or when an industrial shortage threatens defense capabilities.

As inference workloads shift from Ironwood to the new generation, that 80% reduction in performance-per-dollar can allow Google to continue lowering serving costs and help broaden Gemini adoption.
Third-Party TPU Sales Could Become Google’s Next Major Revenue Driver
Second, selling TPUs to third-party customers creates an additional revenue stream for Google outside of monetizing capacity via rentals. Google first made the shift to selling TPUs to third-party customers in April, recognizing the first TPU-related revenue in Q2 at a small scale.
Despite being quite early in this endeavor, its TPU customer list is notable. According to Broadcom’s update in Q3, Anthropic is deploying 1GW of Ironwood TPUs in 2026 , 5GWs of TPU 8i in 2027 and an incremental 10GW in 2028.
Meta signed a multibillion-dollar TPU deal in February and was rumored to explore third-party TPU deployments, while Google’s joint venture with Blackstone aims to bring its first 0.5 GW of TPU capacity online in 2027.
This rapid multi-GW ramp is expected to create a significant revenue stream for Google, with the majority of shipments starting to land in 2027 and beyond. Barclays projects third-party TPU sales could generate roughly $20 billion in revenue in 2026, more than triple to $67 billion in 2027 and more than triple again to $252.7 billion by 2028. This is one of the most aggressive ramps expected, with Piper Sandler and Morgan Stanley projecting just over $100 billion by 2028.
To put this in perspective, that 2028 projection from Barclays would imply that third-party TPU sales would ramp to the current run rate of Google’s Search business in just two and a half years.
Analysts project rapid growth in Google’s third-party TPU business through 2028. Barclays forecasts TPU sales reaching $252.7 billion by 2028, while Piper Sandler and Morgan Stanley estimate approximately $104 billion and $108 billion, respectively. However, this secondary revenue stream currently comes with a fair degree of risk. At the moment, Anthropic accounts for the majority of expected third-party shipments, creating customer concentration and financing risk for TPU shipments.
Merchant TPU Sales Shift the Capex Burden to Customers
This secondary revenue stream from selling TPUs also shifts the capex burden away from Google to its third-party customers. With $200 billion earmarked towards capex this year, any incremental revenue that doesn’t require Google to build the data center first is unusually valuable.
For Google to deploy TPUs through Google Cloud and rent them to customers, it must first pay upfront for the data center, the power, the cooling and the chips, reflected in its 2026 capex bill at $195-205 billion. Once TPUs are installed and energized, Google can then recover its capex by renting these chips out in the cloud across several years.
However, merchant TPU sales to third-party customers flip that equation around. Under this sales model, customers would fund the data center and the hardware purchases, with Google’s revenue coming at chip delivery instead of those multi-year rental terms. Thus, that estimated $253 billion revenue stream would appear in financials as systems are shipped.
One major drawback to shifting capex to its customers is that R&D labs such as Anthropic, who accounts for the majority of projected shipments, do not enjoy the same balance sheet strength as hyperscalers. While Anthropic says it was cash flow positive in Q3, it does not have the same strength in terms of cash as the hyperscalers do. If Google plays a larger role in helping finance chip purchases (where Broadcom currently is active with its AI XPV platform), some of the cash benefit shifts back onto Google’s balance sheet or into contingent obligations.

Google’s Challenge to Nvidia Extends Beyond Inference
While the 8i targets inference workloads, new details on the training-focused TPU 8t show how far Google is pushing scale.
At Hot Chips in August, Google outlined an 8t ‘superpod’ of 9,600 chips with access to 2PB of shared HBM and 121 exaflops of FP4 compute, offering roughly twice the performance-per-watt of Ironwood. The scale is made possible by Google’s optical circuit switches, which can dynamically carve slices out of a 9,600-chip pod. Compare this to Nvidia’s current rack-scale systems, where the scale-up domain tops out at 72 GPUs.
Google is also taking this scale a step further with the introduction of its dedicated Virgo Network fabric. With Virgo, Google can link together fourteen of the ‘superpods’ in a single fabric, capable of supporting 134,400 TPUs in a single domain with 47 PB/s of bandwidth. This cluster size offers 1.6 yottaflops of compute (or 1.6 million exaflops) for massive, large-scale frontier model training.
According to AMD at CES 2026, “Reaching yottascale AI compute would require the equivalent of millions of today’s Frontier exaflop-class supercomputers operating together.”
Nvidia Delay Could Help Google; But it’s Not Necessary
As we’ve said in the past, Nvidia’s product roadmap has consistently pushed the limits across the supply chain. This led to a delay in 2024 on Blackwell, due to the systems overheating.

Factors contributing to the reported delay and TrendForce’s revision include “the time required to validate the newer HBM4 memory used by the chips, challenges with the migration to Nvidia’s faster ConnectX-9 NICs, the system’s higher overall power consumption, and the more advanced liquid cooling requirements.”
Since then, another report has surfaced. In July, CNBC reported that SemiAnalysis found Nvidia’s Kyber rack architecture had been delayed by more than 12 months to 2028, citing a PCB midplane manufacturing issue, though Nvidia has publicly disputed the report.
If these reports prove accurate on the Kyber rack delay, or even if Nvidia shifts the Rubin Ultra architecture from its original planned four-die design to a “2+2” design as rumored, the impacts would be larger than just timing. Kyber marks the system that expands Nvidia’s scale-up domain from 72 GPUs to 576 GPU dies per rack.
A delay to 2028 would mean Nvidia’s largest coherent domain stays in the 72-GPU class through 2027, the same year Google’s merchant TPU revenue is set to ramp with pods of 1,152 chips for inference and 9,600 chips for training (slightly larger than the 9,216-chip pod with Ironwood).
Google is also quickly catching up to Nvidia in terms of unit volume, although a majority of these will be used internally to serve Gemini. However, any potential delay on Nvidia’s roadmap could open the door for further market share gains, as estimated TPU volumes range from 8.8 to 10 million in 2027, above UBS’ estimate for 8.8 million Rubin chips next year.
HBM Supply Constraints Remain the Largest Bottleneck
HBM is the leading constraint the AI industry faces, and commentary from industry executives suggests that the shortage is only worsening with little relief expected in 2027 or even 2028.
In our recent newsletter, Why Nvidia’s SK Hynix Deal Leaves Little AI Memory Supply , we explained that based on industry estimates, Nvidia and Alphabet are expected to demand roughly three-quarters of global HBM supply in 2027. When factoring in AMD’s Helios ramp and numerous custom silicon projects at other hyperscalers, there is little chance for meaningful HBM supply relief or allocation increase.
Faced with such tight supply constraints, even Nvidia, the largest buyer, is paying up to secure access to HBM, and Google likely is having to do the same given its allocation is nearly on par with Nvidia’s, and far ahead of AMD’s.
With the memory market recently shifting in favor of long-term agreements with substantial prepayments, it means cash goes out the door long before TPU revenue is recognized. Customer deposits for third-party TPU capacity can offset this, but it’s a timing drag during a ramp, which is exactly what 2027 will be.
Google’s greatest competitive advantage may not be building a faster chip than Nvidia, but rather in building AI infrastructure at a lower cost. The company has spent years designing accelerators around the demands of its own AI workloads, giving it an advantage that traditional chip companies cannot easily replace.

The Kremlin's seeming interest in negotiations aims not to end the war in Ukraine but rather to secure economic concessions from the United States such that Russia can continue funding its war. Various Russian officials tried to present the October 21 meeting between US Special Representative to the Middle East Steve Witkoff and Russian President Vladimir Putin as evidence that Russia is willing to engage in meaningful negotiations to end the war while simultaneously attempting to extract economic concessions from the United States so that Russia can continue funding the war.[1] Russian Direct Investment Fund (RDIF) CEO Kirill Dmitriev, for example, claimed on October 21 that Witkoff and Putin used the October 21 meeting to agree to expand Russian-US economic cooperation and to “continue communicating.”[2] A Kremlin domestic policy official told Russian opposition source Verstka that the Kremlin does not expect negotiations to resolve the war in Ukraine but that Russian officials hope to extract economic concessions, which could boost Russia’s budget and give Russia a military advantage.[3]
Other Russian officials meanwhile demonstrated Russia’s unwillingness to make any meaningful concessions toward ending the war in Ukraine. Kremlin Spokesperson Dmitry Peskov stated on October 22 that Putin did not present any Russian peace proposal to Witkoff during their meeting and that Putin has not considered the composition of a potential Russian delegation for a future Russia-US-Ukraine trilateral meeting.[4] Russian State Duma deputies expressed skepticism on October 21 and 22 that negotiations could end the war and reiterated claims that freezing the current frontlines would be detrimental to Russia's own efforts to end the war.[5] A source close to the Russian Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MFA) told Verstka on October 22 that the Kremlin has been working for over 1.5 years to try to sway the Trump administration to Russia's side and that the Kremlin's current negotiation efforts are emphasizing Ukraine's alleged hopelessness. These are similar to the Kremlin's earlier negotiation tactics which tried to convince the United States and the West generally that Ukrainian defenses were going to collapse such that Ukraine should preemptively capitulate.[6]
The Kremlin is fundamentally interested only in buying time to resolve the war on the battlefield while Russia simultaneously reaps the benefits of economic cooperation with the United States. State Duma Defense Committee First Deputy Chairperson Alexei Zhuravlyov stated on October 21 that Russia must "press" Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky on the battlefield and force Ukraine to "capitulate."[7] Zhuravlyov added that Russia should not rely on diplomacy with the United States because Russian "successes on the ground” will strengthen Russia’s negotiating position.[8] Russian Presidential Aide Yuriy Ushakov similarly claimed that Russian forces maintain the "complete strategic initiative” and are conducting a "relentless offensive” in all key areas of the frontline.[9]
Russian forces are not winning on the battlefield, however, particularly as Ukrainian forces have stymied the planned Russian offensive to seize Donetsk Oblast. ISW has observed evidence that Russian forces suffered a net loss of 241 square kilometers of territory in Ukraine since September 1, 2026. The average rate of daily Russian advances has significantly declined since 2025, with an average of 0.25 square kilometers per day so far in 2026. Ukrainian forces have, comparatively, been contesting and seizing the operational initiative across several sectors of the frontline throughout 2026, including most recently in the Lyman direction with Ukraine's Operation Vivaldi which liberated over 270 square kilometers of territory and thwarted the Russian military command’s plan to encircle the Fortress Belt.[10] Russian forces are also facing mounting monthly casualties while the Russian recruitment rate continues to fall short of its loss rate.[11] Russian forces have suffered almost 37,000 casualties thus far in October 2026 — much higher than the 31,270 casualties for all of October 2025.[12] The Kremlin may face mounting challenges in the coming months, should Ukraine continue to contest the operational initiative and disrupt Russian offensive plans, potentially further pushing Russian commanders to urge Putin to conduct a mobilization in some form.[13]
The Kremlin is likely actively seeking economic cooperation with the United States as Russia faces significant financial strain. Sources familiar with the matter told Bloomberg on October 22 that even a few more months of continued war could exact lasting financial damage on Russian state-owned oil company Rosneft, as Ukrainian strikes against Rosneft's oil refineries have significantly reduced the company's profits and increased its debt.[14] Bloomberg added that repeated Ukrainian strikes have prevented Rosneft from completing repairs and restarting refinery operations and that strikes against complex secondary units that often include Western-sourced parts, like catalytic crackers, reformers, and hydrocrackers, are particularly difficult to repair. Russia remains highly susceptible to international sanctions that restrict its access to the technologically advanced components needed to repair the equipment damaged after Ukraine’s strikes.[15] The Kremlin is therefore pushing the United States for various forms of economic relief, so that Russia can continue funding its war against Ukraine.[16] The Kremlin has continuously sought to extract economic concessions during negotiations to alleviate the pressure on its oil industry — including restrictions on Ukrainian strikes against refineries and the relaxation of US sanctions against diesel exports — while expressing little real interest in ending the war.[17]
Russian forces conducted a massive missile and drone strike against Ukraine overnight on October 21 to 22 following the October 21 meeting between Witkoff and Putin — a continuation of Russia’s pattern of launching large strike packages in the days before and after major peace talks. The Ukrainian Air Force reported that Russian forces conducted a massive combined missile and drone strike primarily targeting Kyiv City and using an unspecified number of Iskander-M, S-400, Zircon, and Onyx missiles and 210 long-range drones, 110 of which were jet-powered.[18] Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky stated that Russian forces conducted three waves of ballistic missile strikes against Kyiv City using 21 missiles, damaging residential, energy, railway, and commercial infrastructure.[19] Zelensky stated that Russian strikes targeted 12 Ukrainian oblasts, killing at least two people and injuring at least 34 people. Russian forces regularly conduct massive strikes in the days right before and after bilateral and trilateral talks, such as the August 2025 US-Russia Alaska summit, the November 2025 US-Ukraine talks in Geneva, the November 2025 US-Russia talks in Moscow, the December 2025 US-Ukrainian-European and US-Russian talks, and the January-February 2026 US-Ukrainian-Russian talks in Abu Dhabi.[20]
Russian forces struck Kyiv City with cluster munitions to purposely harm the civilian population, likely as part of wider Russian efforts to distract from Ukraine’s recent battlefield successes and ultimately force Ukraine into capitulation by making daily life untenable. Zelensky stated that Russian forces struck Pechersk Raion, the historic center of Kyiv City, with three ballistic missiles with cluster warheads that have a dispersion diameter of 400 meters.[21] The strikes damaged monastery buildings and other civilian infrastructure near the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra and the Holy Presentation Monastery. Zelensky noted that there were no military targets in the area and that the objective of the strikes was to cause widespread destruction.[22] Kyiv City Mayor Vitaliy Klitschko stated that Russian forces also struck metro infrastructure on the Southern Bridge over the Dnipro River in Kyiv City and damaged six ambulances at an ambulance station in the city.[23] Ukrainian Presidential Advisor on Defense Technologies Serhiy “Flash” Beskrestnov stated that each Iskander-M ballistic missile’s cluster warhead releases 45 submunitions that detonate in the air and noted that the objective of the strikes was to destroy a portion of the historic center and injure as many people as possible.[24] Cluster munitions have a wide area of effect and are designed to kill enemy personnel or destroy light vehicles, underscoring Russia’s intent to maximize civilian harm and suffering. Cluster munitions historically have a high failure rate, and unexploded bomblets can cause lasting danger for civilians living in affected areas, further demonstrating Russia‘s aim to terrorize civilians.[25]
Russian strikes against Kyiv City are intended to make daily life untenable heading into Winter 2026-2027. Zelensky stated that Russian forces have struck more than 170 Ukrainian energy facilities since October 11 — the date when US President Donald Trump announced that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an “energy ceasefire.”[26] The increasing intensity and brutality of Russia's long-range strike campaign ahead of Winter 2026-2027 highlights the urgency of providing Ukraine with sufficient air defense munitions, reliable low-latency satellite access over Russia, and other Western assistance that allows Ukraine to defend its skies from Russian missiles and drones.

The Ukrainian Khartyia Corps reported on October 21 that its Operation Veles is clearing a pipeline near Kupyansk that Russian forces use to infiltrate into Kupyansk, likely referring to the Trans-Balkan pipeline.[35] The success of Operation Veles may allow Ukrainian forces to clear Kupyansk of Russian infiltrators. The Khartiya Corps reported on October 22 that Ukrainian forces combined this underground offensive with ground operations and drone strikes, advancing more than 15 kilometers through the pipeline into the Russian near rear and potentially denying Russian forces the ability to conduct infiltration missions in the Kupyansk direction in the future.[36] The Khartiya Corps spokesperson reported that the pipeline runs two to four meters underground and is between one and 1.2 meters high.[37] The spokesperson stated that Russian servicemembers maintained positions in the tunnels for more than a month building barricades and awaiting further orders from the Russian military command.[38] Ukrainian Joint Forces Task Force Spokesperson Colonel Viktor Trehubov stated on October 22 that Russian forces used a pipeline, which travels north from Kupyansk and across the Oskil River through Holubivka (northeast of Kupyansk), to move covertly into Kupyansk and evacuate Russian troops and sometimes used civilians as human shields in these efforts.[39] Trehubov stated that the main objectives of the Ukrainian operation were to deny Russian forces the ability to covertly enter and exit the areas north of Kupyansk and across the Oskil River, as Ukrainian forces could previously only exert partial control over the pipeline’s exit. Ukrainian forces' various efforts to eliminate Russian forces' use of the pipelines were unsuccessful such that Ukrainian forces have now turned to fighting underground. Russian forces have been using pipelines in the Kupyansk direction as their primary supply lines and infiltration routes since at least September 2025.[41] The Wall Street Journal reported on October 22 that Russian forces cut holes in tunnels and dug their way to the surface in order to launch drones against Ukrainian positions near Kupyansk.[42] Ukrainian forces previously struck these pipelines with drones, dropped munitions onto the pipelines with heavy bomber drones, and used unmanned ground vehicles (UGVs) to bring explosives to the area in order to disrupt Russian activity in the pipeline.[43] The Khartiya Corps spokesperson stated that Ukrainian strikes did not fully prevent Russian forces from using the pipelines, however, because Russian forces would dig into the soil and cut new entries into the tunnels, forcing Ukrainian personnel to themselves go underground to physically push Russians from the pipeline.[44]

Ukraine's Main Military Intelligence Directorate (GUR) reported on October 22 that Russian forces began to use the new Filin cruise missile in late Summer 2026.[64] The GUR reported that the Filin cruise missile has a 3.5-meter-long carbon-fiber and fiberglass composite fuselage, a three-meter wingspan, and some 3D-printed parts and may have a maximum speed of 800 kilometers per hour and a range of up to 1,200 kilometers. The GUR reported that the Filin cruise missile comes in variants with 50- and 100-kilogram warheads (similar to the payload of a Geran-type drone) and launches from a ground launcher using a solid-propellent booster. The GUR noted that the Filin cruise missile uses a Chinese-made inertial navigation system and a 16-channel Kometa controlled reception pattern antenna (CPRA) for targeting and has a Russian R500 engine. The Russian development of the Filin cruise missile indicates Russia’s continued focus on producing scalable and more cost-effective ground- and air-launched cruise missiles that are difficult for Ukrainian forces to intercept at scale.

Russian Defense Minister Andrei Belousov met with Belarusian Defense Minister Lieutenant General Viktor Khrenin on October 22 to discuss deeper military cooperation.[65] Belousov and Khrenin met in Minsk, Belarus to discuss the coordination of military command bodies, cooperation between defense industries, joint air defense, and opportunities for the Russian and Belarusian militaries to conduct joint training. Belousov stated that both states will continue to work to create a unified defense and security state. Increased military cooperation between the Russian and Belarusian militaries is part of Russia’s efforts to maintain its de facto annexation of Belarus, as ISW has long assessed.[66]

An oil spill from a tanker attacked near the Strait of Hormuz is spreading along the United Arab Emirates (UAE) coastline, forcing the closure of some beaches and coastal areas.
Satellite images from Monday show the beginnings of the oil spill from a ship matching the location and dimensions of Gem No.2, which was damaged between 9 and 10 October during a record surge of vessel strikes by Iran .
The slick of oil had spread more than 35km (22 miles) from the tanker by Wednesday, the satellite imagery showed.
UAE authorities said on Thursday that the incident is affecting beaches near the northern city of Umm Al Quwain and verified video from Friday shows the oil washing ashore, colouring the water black.
The UAE&#x27;s Ministry of Climate Change and Environment said on Thursday that it "immediately began containment" in order to "limit environmental impacts".
Prof Steve Widdicombe, director of Sharjah Marine Science Research Centre in the UAE, told BBC Verify it was "inevitable that there will be an impact", but researchers caution it is too soon to assess extent of the damage.
Initially a much smaller oil leak could be seen emerging from the ship on 19 October.
But the slick appears to have rapidly spread over two days, according to radar satellite imagery reviewed by BBC Verify and intelligence firm MAIAR.
Oil spills can cause immediate deaths of marine animals and other organisms, for example by physically trapping turtles, smothering the feathers of seabirds or damaging the gills of fish.
They can also cause longer-lasting damage, Prof Widdicombe said, if species remain exposed to toxic compounds from the oil which could impact their ability to reproduce.
Researchers have cautioned the environmental impact at this stage is very uncertain, as it depends on factors including the volume and type of oil spilled, how quickly it evaporates or breaks down and the effectiveness of the clean-up operation.
But the spill does extend towards Siniyah and Khor Al Beidah islands, a globally recognised Key Biodiversity Area containing habitats like corals, seagrasses and saltmarshes, as well as species including green turtles and the critically endangered Halavi guitarfish.
The proximity of the spill to this area is the "most concerning environmental angle", according to Eoghan Darbyshire, a scientist at the Conflict and Environment Observatory.
BBC Verify analysis of the dimensions and location of the ship indicate they match a crude oil tanker called Gem No.2.
Maritime intelligence firm Vanguard reported the Panama-flagged tanker was "struck by an unknown projectile" on 9 October, just north of the town of Al Jazeera Al Hamra, causing a fire on board that was later extinguished.
Verified footage first posted on social media on 10 October showed the crew running across the deck and putting on lifejackets as a large cloud of black smoke rose over the ship.
Iranian state media shared footage of a ship matching Gem No.2 which appeared to show the vessel on fire with burning oil spilling into the sea.
The ship had been attempting to exit the Strait of Hormuz via an "unauthorized and dangerous route", according to state media.
All 22 Indian crew members were rescued, the Indian consulate in Dubai said on 11 October.
Gem No.2 is one of a record number of ships that reported being attacked in and around the Strait of Hormuz at the start of October .
Security experts told BBC Verify this was part of an Iranian effort to assert greater control over the strait, in response to the US military successfully escorting more vessels through the vital shipping lane.
Tankers and other commercial vessels have come under attack from both Iranian and US forces in and around the Strait of Hormuz amid the conflict between the two countries.
