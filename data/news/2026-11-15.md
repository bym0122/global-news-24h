Warren Buffett warns investors against speculative stock trading
During an interview with CNBC around Berkshire Hathaway's 2026 annual meeting, Buffett reiterated concerns about investors treating financial markets as a venue for short-term speculation rather than long-term business ownership (see Warren Buffett Retirement Income Strategy : Does the 90/10 Rule Still Work?).
"I've compared the markets to a church with a casino attached," Buffett said, distinguishing investment based on business fundamentals from trading driven primarily by price movements.
He warned that speculative activity had become particularly attractive, encouraging some market participants to accept risks without sufficient regard for underlying valuations. "That's not investing, it's not speculating, it's gambling," Buffett said.

Buffett also distinguished between excessive prices and the long-term value of owning productive businesses. He suggested that valuations across parts of the market could eventually appear difficult to justify.
"That doesn't mean that investing is terrible. It does mean that prices for an awful lot of things will look very silly."
The distinction matters because even companies with strong earnings growth can generate disappointing investment returns when their shares are purchased at excessive valuations.
Shiller CAPE ratio exceeds 40 as US stock valuations climb
The S&P 500 has recorded more than two dozen all-time highs during 2026, contributing to historically elevated valuation multiples.
One of the most widely followed measures is the Shiller CAPE ratio, which compares the inflation-adjusted price of the S&P 500 with the average inflation-adjusted earnings of its constituent companies over the preceding ten years.
Unlike conventional price-to-earnings ratios, CAPE uses a decade of earnings to reduce the influence of short-term changes in corporate profitability.
A reading above 40 indicates that investors are paying more than 40 times the index's average real earnings over that period.
Such valuations imply substantial expectations for future earnings growth and can leave share prices more sensitive to disappointing results, changes in interest rates or shifts in investor sentiment.
The Shiller CAPE ratio is a measure of valuation risk, not a market-timing indicator. It does not identify when prices will peak or how quickly a correction might develop.
High valuations can also reflect structural changes in the economy, including differences in corporate profitability, sector composition and investor expectations.
The increasing weight of large technology companies in major US indices is particularly relevant when comparing current valuation levels with earlier market cycles.
What the 1929 crash, dot-com bubble and 2022 bear market reveal
Historically, unusually high CAPE readings have coincided with periods preceding major stock market declines.
Before the 1929 Wall Street crash, US equities traded at elevated valuations as speculative demand pushed share prices substantially higher.
The subsequent market collapse helped mark the beginning of the Great Depression, although the economic downturn involved multiple financial and monetary factors beyond stock valuations.
During the late 1990s, enthusiasm for internet companies drove the CAPE ratio above 40.
The dot-com bubble subsequently burst, with technology stocks experiencing particularly severe losses as investors reassessed business models, earnings expectations and valuations.
Another significant valuation peak occurred in late 2021, shortly before US equities entered a bear market in 2022.
Rising interest rates, inflation and tighter monetary policy contributed to that downturn. These episodes demonstrate that high initial valuations can increase exposure to losses when market conditions deteriorate. They do not establish a consistent interval between a valuation peak and the beginning of a decline.
Markets have also remained expensive for years without immediately entering a sustained downturn.
Why high stock market valuations matter for future returns
The relationship between starting valuations and subsequent investment performance is generally more informative over long periods than over individual months.
When investors purchase shares at unusually high earnings multiples, future returns depend partly on companies generating sufficient profit growth to justify those prices.
If earnings fail to meet expectations, valuation multiples may contract even when businesses remain profitable.
This creates two separate risks: lower share prices during a market correction and weaker investment returns over subsequent years.
However, valuation measures cannot account for every factor influencing market performance. Corporate earnings, productivity, economic growth, monetary policy and investor demand can all affect equity prices.
For investors with long-term retirement accounts, elevated valuations are therefore a reason to examine portfolio concentration and risk exposure rather than assume that a market decline is imminent.
Buffett's warning highlights the difference between price and value
Buffett's investment philosophy has historically emphasized the relationship between the price paid for a security and the underlying economic value of the business.
His latest warning focuses on the risks of abandoning that distinction when markets are rising rapidly.
The Shiller CAPE ratio provides additional historical context by showing how expensive US equities have become relative to long-term corporate earnings.
Neither Buffett's remarks nor the valuation indicator establishes that a crash is inevitable in the near term.
The central risk is that investors paying unusually high prices may have less protection against earnings disappointments or changes in market sentiment, particularly when their portfolios are concentrated in stocks with elevated valuations.

However, the S&P 500 index lost 9.1% over the following 12 months, as inflation remained high and the central bank accelerated rate increases. The Russia-Ukraine war, slowing global growth, and recession fears also weighed on stocks.
These examples show why the market's immediate reaction to a rate hike does not necessarily indicate how stocks will perform over the following year.

Prior to the trade restrictions, the Strait of Hormuz was used to transport around one-fifth of the world’s oil and liquefied natural gas (LNG).
In 2025, roughly 20 million barrels of oil and oil products passed through the waterway every day, equivalent to almost $600 billion in energy trade per year, according to estimates from the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA). As Iran continues to impose trade restrictions, several countries have been forced to halt energy exports , particularly in the Persian Gulf, resulting in a severe negative impact on certain economies.

The recent trade restrictions have exposed vulnerabilities in global energy trade and demonstrated the importance of diversification – both in energy exports and in expanding production beyond fossil fuels to renewable alternatives.

Ukraine applied to become a NATO member in September 2022, just months after Russia’s full-scale invasion of the country.
Since then, Zelenskyy, his officials and Ukrainians around the world have been lobbying NATO nations to speed up Kyiv’s accession process.

NATO members bordering Russia, like Lithuania and Poland, have been most enthusiastic about backing Ukraine’s ambitions. Several other nations in the alliance have also been supportive of an expedited process for Kyiv to join, amid concerns about corruption in Ukraine and while seeking an end to the war with Russia as a condition for the country to become a NATO member.

Yet, while the new proposal from Witkoff and Kushner revives Ukraine’s hopes of joining NATO, it demands a compromise that no government in Kyiv can easily make.
On Saturday, Zelenskyy told reporters that the prospect of Ukraine joining NATO in exchange for ceding territory had not been raised by members of the alliance before and reiterated that Kyiv would not give up any of its territories.
But he said if Ukraine received a peace deal which included security guarantees, the right to maintain a strong and well-funded Ukrainian army, and funds to rebuild the country, then he would hold a national referendum on the question of ceding territory in exchange for NATO membership.

But it has maintained a strict position that Ukraine’s NATO membership is a red line for Moscow and a direct threat to Russia’s national security. In fact, Putin has also cited the issue of Ukraine’s NATO aspirations as one reason why Russia launched its war in 2022.
Despite Kushner and Witkoff’s meetings with Putin, the Kremlin has shown little appetite for a change in that position. Moscow has continued, throughout the war, to demand that Ukraine abandon its NATO ambitions and maintain a neutral and demilitarised status. Russia has also firmly opposed any deployment of Western troops or peacekeepers on Ukrainian soil.
In addition to demanding that Ukraine give up its NATO dreams, Moscow has also insisted that Ukraine should cede sovereignty over the Donbas region, in addition to parts of Zaporizhia and Kherson provinces that Russia already occupies.
Just as the new US proposal asks Ukraine to give up some territory in return for a NATO seat, it offers Russia a bargain — withdraw its red line on Ukraine’s NATO membership and get the Donbas in exchange.
The region in eastern Ukraine has historically been the country’s industrial heartland and is therefore a critical economic engine.
A majority of people in the Donbas identify as ethnically Ukrainian, but a minority identifies as ethnically Russian. The Russian language is also widely spoken in the region — a legacy of Russian migration there in the 19th century and then under the Soviet Union.
Russia accuses Ukraine of discriminating against Russian-speaking and ethnically Russian people in the Donbas and has used this claim to justify its demand that Kyiv hand over the region to Moscow.
The region consists of the provinces of Luhansk and Donetsk. Since the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, Russia has occupied almost all of Luhansk and most of Donetsk. In all, it controls about 88 percent of the Donbas region. But it has consistently demanded that Ukrainian troops withdraw from the remaining parts of the Donbas they currently hold.

“They [the Russians] want to discuss what they call a new European security architecture, which is a code name for Russia having veto [power] when it comes to NATO,” he said.
He noted that it was also interesting that the Americans did not put forward the idea of Kyiv ceding the Donbas to the Russians in exchange for NATO membership.
“But everything indicates that the Russians will never accept this,” he said, referring to the idea of Ukraine joining NATO.
“The Russians have no intention of actually coming to the negotiating table. They’re just bringing up new issues [currently about European security architecture] and broadening the agenda in order to make it look like they’re negotiating and appease the American leadership, while inflicting as much damage on Ukraine as possible,” he added.
The US has not explicitly announced that it has suggested Kyiv should cede territory for NATO membership.

How the Nvidia Chip Loophole Works: Renting Versus Shipping
US export controls restrict the sale and transfer of advanced AI chips to Chinese buyers. They focus on hardware moving across borders. The loophole that DW and other outlets have examined exploits that focus. A Chinese company does not need to import a GPU if it can rent time on one that sits in a data center in another country. The chip never crosses into China, but the compute still serves a Chinese customer.
Reports describe Chinese AI labs and large technology companies renting capacity from overseas providers. Alibaba and Tencent appear in reporting as end users served by some of the chips, along with smaller Chinese AI laboratories. Whether this kind of renting violates US rules depends on how the rules are written and enforced, and the reporting treats it largely as a gap rather than a clear violation. Claims that Alibaba or other named Chinese companies knowingly took part in illegal diversion remain unconfirmed.
The second route is a different matter. Diversion means buying chips through a front or intermediary and then moving them to a buyer who could not have bought them directly. It is the conduct US investigators look for when they examine a cloud customer. The investigation into Megaspeed concerns alleged diversion or smuggling of restricted semiconductors. Megaspeed and related parties deny any connection to it.
The distinction matters for readers who see headlines that say "Nvidia chips smuggled to China." Hosting chips abroad and renting them out is an ordinary business, even when the renter is Chinese. Moving restricted chips into China without a license is a violation. Mixing the two lets people claim too much in either direction.

Historical Context: Why Export Controls Target Hardware
The remote-access gap exists because of how Washington built its restrictions. The earliest controls on advanced AI chips targeted physical products. Officials could count GPUs, set performance thresholds, and require licenses for shipments. That approach worked reasonably well when the main worry was a Chinese buyer importing a server rack.
Cloud computing changed the picture. A GPU cluster in another country can serve users anywhere, and the service moves through ordinary internet connections. The law has to decide whether renting access counts as an export of the capability. The Megaspeed episode puts that question back on the table with a high-profile example.
A second pressure point came from enforcement. Controls on paper depend on customs checks, license reviews, and company-level compliance. Each layer has limits, and a determined intermediary can exploit the seams between them.
