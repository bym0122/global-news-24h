He never mentions the man who started it: President Donald Trump .
It&#x27;s a dramatic reversal for Rogers, who spent months defending Trump&#x27;s tariffs , saying as recently as September that " Trump is right to put America first " by imposing duties. WASHINGTON (TNND) — Surging prices for energy and other goods sparked renewed pressure on prices last month, pushing inflation to a recent high and raising questions about how long the Federal Reserve can afford to wait before raising interest rates again.
Consumer prices climbed 3.7% in September compared to the year prior, up from 3.4% in August. On a monthly basis, inflation accelerated by 0.6%, the biggest monthly jump since the early days of the war with Iran.
The continued climb in prices highlights the ongoing impact the war with Iran has had on energy prices around the world and the growing risks higher fuel costs will spread throughout the economy. Prices also climbed across other categories that are impacted by fuel costs. Airfare jumped 22% from a year earlier, while costs for apparel and toys that are sensitive to shipment costs and tariffs also moved higher.
The longer energy prices are elevated, the greater the risk of businesses passing higher transportation and production costs onto consumers, creating the risk of an energy shock becoming a more persistent source of inflation.
Core prices, which exclude food and energy, increased by 2.6% year-over-year and 0.3% from a month earlier. A continued increase in core inflation is a sign price pressures are expanding beyond areas that are more sensitive to swings in energy prices.
“The core inflation rate is more concerning, as it is more informative of the larger trends in the economy.
