UK asks Interpol to reject Argentina’s ‘politically motivated’ requests for Falklands oil arrests
Exclusive: Caution urged over Javier Milei government’s ‘red notice’ requests for arrest of individuals linked to oil project
Britain has appealed to Interpol to reject Argentina’s requests for the arrest of individuals involved in a large oil project off the Falkland Islands .
With tensions over the sovereignty of the British overseas territory reaching levels not seen since the war with Argentina in 1982, UK officials have sought urgent assurances from the international organisation.
A letter has been sent to Interpol’s headquarters in Lyon arguing that “red notice” requests from Argentina for the arrest of such individuals, including British nationals, should be seen as politically motivated.
It is understood the UK government is concerned by what sources suggested was an attempt to bully people and companies out of working in the Falklands .
An oil project known as Sea Lion in the North Falkland Basin in the south Atlantic has the potential to triple the Falklands’ GDP but Argentina’s president, Javier Milei , has vowed to block it. Argentina’s strategy includes attempts to criminalise those involved in Sea Lion and deter other investors.
In its letter, the UK government has urged Interpol to be cautious in its handling of Argentinian requests for detentions, it is understood.
Andrea Clausen, the chief executive of the Falklands government, confirmed that the use of Interpol by Argentina was a threat but added: “One would hope that Interpol wouldn’t necessarily take action.”

Interpol screens red notices, which are requests to locate and provisionally arrest a person pending extradition. If circulated to the enforcement agencies, individual member countries then decide whether to act on the requests. Nearly 20,000 were issued last year.
The foreign secretary, Ed Miliband, has told MPs that the UK will protect businesses working in the Falklands from pressure from Argentina through measures under protection of trading interests (PTI) legislation.
The legislation allows the UK to issue orders and directions forbidding British individuals and companies from complying with specific foreign laws or trade controls. It also opens up the possibility that those affected by Argentinian legal pressure could claim damages in UK courts.
