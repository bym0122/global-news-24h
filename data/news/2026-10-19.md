Hormuz Is Turning Shipping’s Fuel Bill Into a Supply Crisis
The Hormuz crisis is tightening global bunker fuel supplies, with disrupted Middle Eastern and Russian exports driving shortages and longer delivery times.
Soaring marine fuel prices are squeezing shipowners, increasing voyage costs, working capital requirements, and pressure on profit margins.
Global shipping faces growing disruption, as unreliable refueling, limited inventories, and tighter credit threaten trade routes and vessel schedules.
The Hormuz crisis is not only hitting crude oil and gas, but also container shipping. It is also tightening its grip on global shipping through the fuel tanks of vessels carrying world trade. Disrupted exports, constrained refinery supplies, and increasingly unreliable deliveries are clearly at present pushing the bunker market into a dangerous position. Fuel remains available internationally, but securing the right grade at the right port within a commercially workable window is becoming harder. The consequences will be severe, especially for shipowners, as it extends beyond expensive bunkers. Delayed deliveries and uncertain replenishment are not only threatening voyage schedules but also working capital and the profitability of entire trading routes.
This pressure is measurable. On October 16, Vitol estimated that high-sulfur fuel oil flows through Hormuz, including Iranian supplies, remained at least 800,000 tons per month below February levels. At the same time, the successful attacks by Ukraine on Russian refineries have removed an additional estimated million tons monthly. Meanwhile, North Asian refiners were drawing more than a million tons of HSFO for November processing. Global shipping is therefore competing for a constrained product pool. This is a growing reality, even though low-sulfur supplies have received some support from increased exports from China and Nigeria.

These pressures are now converging on a market that depends as much on reliable distribution as on aggregate production. A ton of fuel awaiting export, committed to another customer or stranded behind a disrupted shipping route cannot satisfy an immediate bunker requirement. The market still doesn't seem to recognize this fully. Refinery output, export capacity, blending components, storage and barge availability must all align before a vessel can refuel. Hormuz has weakened several of those links at once, allowing disruption in one location to ripple through the wider supply chain. The spreading of conflict in the Middle East, especially in and around the Bab El Mandeb and Saudi Arabia, is only increasing the impact.
Fujairah illustrates both the market’s adaptability and its continuing vulnerability. Reuters reported on October 1 that third-quarter fuel oil imports had recovered to 2.6 million tons from 845,000 tons in the second quarter. Nevertheless, September imports remained approximately half February’s level. Yes, supplies were returning, but through a system still operating well below earlier import volumes. A partial recovery in cargo movements cannot establish dependable bunker availability when replenishment remains exposed to renewed interruption. The current security situation in and around Hormuz and the Gulf of Oman doesn’t bode well.
The latest delivery assessments show how uneven that recovery remains. ENGINE’s October 13 outlook put Singapore’s recommended very-low-sulfur fuel oil lead times at 11–18 days, compared with 10–15 days a week earlier. HSFO required 11–17 days. Fujairah’s VLSFO availability had improved, while HSFO and low-sulfur marine gasoil remained limited. Key Omani ports offered a more comfortable marine gasoil position, with one supplier quoting one to two days. The emerging crisis is geographically fragmented, with pressure varying by fuel grade and delivery location.
That fragmentation complicates procurement, without making the commercial damage less serious. A ship requiring fuel within three days cannot necessarily absorb a two-week booking window. Physical shortages become commercially disruptive well before a port exhausts its stocks. The critical threshold will be crossed when available supply can no longer accommodate the schedules on which shipping contracts depend.
Inventory figures can consequently offer misleading reassurance. ENGINE reported that Fujairah’s heavy distillate and residual stocks averaged 4.72 million barrels in early October, approximately 5% above September’s average. At the same time, local bunker availability remains severely constrained. Those inventories do not all represent uncommitted, immediately deliverable marine fuel. Product specifications, ownership, and export obligations determine how much can actually reach a vessel. More barrels in storage can coexist with fewer practical purchasing options for an owner facing a departure deadline.
Here, the market and market participants play a pivotal role. The economics of holding inventory add another vulnerability. S&P Global reported in September that strong backwardation, with prompt fuel priced above later deliveries, was discouraging blenders from maintaining stocks. Blending closer to delivery reduced their exposure to falling prices. However, it also increases sensitivity to delayed or unsuitable components. Across a stressed market, individually rational decisions can remove the collective buffer needed to absorb disruption. We are now looking at a situation in which a replenishment delay that would previously have been manageable can then translate directly into a failed delivery.
At the same time, shippers are looking at financial constraints too. Higher prices are already imposing a substantial financing burden. General Index assessed September VLSFO averages at $872 per ton in Singapore and $939 in Fujairah, up 6% and 15.9%, respectively, from August. An additional $100 per ton increases the cost of a 2,000-ton purchase by $200,000, before accounting for waiting time or diversion expenses. Shippers will calculate all of this; if it repeats across a fleet, such increases demand considerably more cash to maintain existing operations. It seems the media still doesn’t understand that even strong headline freight earnings don't automatically translate into stronger margins.
The next constraint could emerge through credit. When fuel prices rise, an unchanged financing facility buys fewer tons. Suppliers need additional funding to maintain throughput, while owners need more liquidity to cover each voyage. If lenders or counterparties tighten terms, physical availability may be accompanied by financial exclusion: fuel can be purchased, but only by buyers able to provide sufficient cash or security. Smaller operators would be particularly vulnerable to this combination of expensive deliveries and reduced purchasing flexibility.
Owners are already adjusting. Reuters reported on October 16 that bunker purchases were becoming smaller and more frequent, with shorter contractual commitments and greater use of alternative locations, including Port Louis, Colombo, and Salalah. This not only helps operators manage immediate exposure but also shifts demand toward ports that must secure additional replenishment and delivery capacity. Geographic diversification improves options; its effectiveness still depends on the fuel and infrastructure available behind each alternative call.
Over the coming weeks, regional shortages will recur, premiums will remain volatile, and booking requirements will lengthen. A broader crisis will occur if additional export losses coincide with refinery outages and reduced tanker availability.
Shipping does not need every bunker hub to run dry for the damage to become systemic. Schedules will be disrupted if persistent uncertainty over delivery remains in place. Hormuz is steadily turning dependable refueling into a more expensive and conditional service. Customers far beyond the Gulf will be feeling the consequences.

On October 19, ahead of the China A-share market open, a dense batch of policy and earnings news landed. China&#x27;s leadership designated computing power networks as one of the "six networks" in national planning and construction, and the State Council Executive Meeting called for deepening the "AI+" initiative and pushing technology deployment with a focus on the real economy. China&#x27;s Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology proposed adding 40 technologies in artificial intelligence, biopharmaceuticals and solar power to the export-restriction list, with temporary registration requirements. The 2026 Financial Street Forum Annual Conference opens the same day, with heads of the People&#x27;s Bank of China, the National Financial Regulatory Administration, the China Securities Regulatory Commission and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange slated to deliver keynote speeches. On earnings, CXMT expects first-three-quarter net profit of 131 billion to 132.5 billion yuan (about $19.6 billion to $19.8 billion), a turnaround from a year-earlier loss, and its chairman proposed a 1 billion to 2 billion yuan buyback. AI supply chain companies including Shengyi Technology, Demingli, Hengtong Optic-Electric and Wellyhigh reported sharp profit increases. Weakness in U.S. and Japanese-South Korean semiconductor stocks may weigh on opening sentiment.

Ahead of the October 19 open, China's A-share market is absorbing a dense flow of policy and earnings information. On one side, China's top leadership issued new directives on computing infrastructure and the "AI+" initiative, while the Financial Street Forum Annual Conference opens in Beijing the same day, with the heads of four financial regulators set to speak collectively. On the other, AI supply chain companies spanning memory, copper-clad laminates and PCBs (printed circuit boards) clustered in disclosing first-three-quarter earnings forecasts, with several posting turnarounds or multi-fold growth. CXMT drew the spotlight with a cumulative profit forecast of more than 130 billion yuan (about $19.5 billion).
On policy, Xi Jinping, general secretary of the Communist Party of China Central Committee and president of China, said during an inspection tour on October 16 in Ulanqab, Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, that computing power networks are one of the "six networks" in national planning and construction, and that the computing power industry is in a phase of accelerated development with broad prospects. He called for implementing requirements for efficiency, green development and security, emphasizing "computing-power and electricity coordination" and "computing-power and water coordination," building and operating computing center projects well, and actively promoting the development of green computing power. The State Council Executive Meeting held the same day, chaired by Premier Li Qiang, noted that artificial intelligence is a key variable shaping future development and called for using deployment applications to pull technological innovation, deepening and expanding the "AI+" initiative, strengthening supply-demand linkage with a focus on the real economy, advancing pilot-scale validation, and expanding application scenarios so that AI technology translates more effectively into real productivity.
On external technology management, China's Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology opened a public consultation on revisions to the Catalogue of Technologies Prohibited or Restricted from Export, proposing to add 40 technologies across artificial intelligence, biopharmaceuticals and solar power, and to impose temporary registration management on exports of the relevant technologies. The AI category covers 23 technologies related to large models, intelligent agents, computing clusters and information processing; technologies already in the public domain through open-source release or academic publication would not be covered. The biopharmaceutical category covers five technologies related to cell and gene therapy, small nucleic acid drugs and pathogenic microorganisms, with outbound licensing models that do not involve ownership transfer eligible for facilitation measures such as automatic licensing. The solar category covers 12 technologies related to polysilicon manufacturing, wafer manufacturing and cell manufacturing, along with key core equipment embodying those technologies.
On finance, the 2026 Financial Street Forum Annual Conference runs from October 19 to 22 at Beijing's Financial Street under the theme "Openness, Cooperation, Inclusiveness — Building a New Ecosystem for Global Financial Development," drawing more than 400 guests from over 40 countries and regions. Pan Gongsheng, governor of the People's Bank of China; Ding Xiangqun, head of the National Financial Regulatory Administration; Wu Qing, chairman of the China Securities Regulatory Commission; and Zhu Hexin, head of the State Administration of Foreign Exchange, will attend and deliver keynote speeches. Separately, the People's Bank of China opened a public consultation on Rules for Bond Issuance Management in the Interbank Bond Market, specifying that issue prices, interest rates and commission fees should be determined on a market basis, and prohibiting conduct that affects fair bond pricing or involves improper transfer of benefits.
On industry regulation, four Chinese ministries — the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, the Ministry of Public Security, the Ministry of Ecology and Environment and the State Administration for Market Regulation — organized a discussion with more than 30 major automakers, requiring them to firmly resist irrational competition, comprehensively strengthen testing and validation, truthfully publicize the usage boundaries and limitations of innovative product designs, refrain from false, exaggerated or misleading advertising, and avoid inducing drivers to neglect safe-driving responsibilities. Next steps include accelerating the revision of relevant standards, continuing supervisory inspections of major automakers and publicly exposing problematic companies. The National Development and Reform Commission, the National Energy Administration and other departments, meanwhile, held a meeting in Taiyuan, Shanxi, on accelerating the resumption and increase of coal mine production to ensure supply, requiring "one mine, one policy" restart plans and pushing temporarily suspended mines to resume operations quickly and reach full capacity as soon as possible.
Earnings are the most concentrated focus ahead of the open. CXMT announced it expects first-three-quarter 2026 revenue of about 265 billion to 270 billion yuan (about $39.7 billion to $40.4 billion), an increase of 232.916 billion to 237.916 billion yuan from a year earlier, and net profit attributable to shareholders of about 131 billion to 132.5 billion yuan (about $19.6 billion to $19.8 billion), compared with a loss of 5.28 billion yuan (about $790.9 million) a year earlier — a swing to profit. The company attributed the results to AI computing demand growth and improving DRAM industry conditions, which left products in short supply and drove sharp price increases. The same day, the board received a letter from Chairman Zhu Yiming proposing a share buyback of 1 billion to 2 billion yuan (about $149.8 million to $299.6 million) through centralized bidding at a price no higher than 150% of the average trading price over the 30 trading days before the board resolution approving the buyback, with repurchased shares intended for employee equity incentives, employee stock ownership plans or reduction of registered capital.
Other companies in the supply chain also posted strong results. Shengyi Technology expects first-three-quarter net profit attributable to shareholders of 6.46 billion to 6.87 billion yuan (about $967.7 million to $1.0 billion), up 164% to 181% year on year, mainly on higher copper-clad laminate sales and increased revenue and gross profit, with its circuit board segment benefiting from the AI computing boom. Demingli expects first-three-quarter revenue of 27.5 billion to 29.5 billion yuan (about $4.1 billion to $4.4 billion), up 312.97% to 343.00%, and net profit attributable to shareholders of 8.65 billion to 8.95 billion yuan (about $1.3 billion), versus a loss of 27.0765 million yuan (about $4.1 million) a year earlier, with enterprise storage revenue and its share of the total rising further. Hengtong Optic-Electric expects first-three-quarter net profit of 5.97 billion to 6.37 billion yuan (about $894.3 million to $954.2 million), up 151.27% to 168.10%.

On quarterly performance, Sihui Fuji posted third-quarter revenue of 857 million yuan (about $128.4 million), up 59.78% year on year, and net profit of 176 million yuan (about $26.4 million), up 217.22%, mainly driven by order growth in industrial control, automotive electronics and communications. On buybacks and capital moves, Cheng Yuhua, an independent director of Montage Technology, proposed a buyback of 3 billion to 5 billion yuan (about $44.9 million to $74.9 million) of A-shares using the company's own funds for an employee stock ownership plan or equity incentives. The chairman of Dawning Information Industry proposed a buyback of 3 billion to 5 billion yuan, the chairman of Hygon Information Technology proposed 5 billion to 8 billion yuan (about $74.9 million to $119.8 million), and the chairman of Shengyi Technology proposed 2 billion to 3 billion yuan (about $30.0 million to $44.9 million). Shenghe Resources plans to transfer 51% stakes in four rare earth smelting and separation subsidiaries, including Leshan Shenghe, to China Rare Earth Group Industrial Development Co., with the targets valued at a combined 3.25 billion to 3.55 billion yuan (about $486.8 million to $531.8 million), corresponding to a transaction price estimated at 1.6575 billion to 1.8105 billion yuan (about $248.3 million to $271.2 million); after the deal, the four companies will no longer be consolidated. Lakala plans to acquire a controlling stake in Guangzhou Gaofu Information Technology Co. through a share issuance and cash payment, with an issue price of 12.88 yuan per share; the target's "Skyee" platform provides cross-border fund settlement services for cross-border e-commerce and foreign trade companies, covering cross-border payments in 175 countries and regions. Lakala's shares will resume trading at the October 19 open.
The external market backdrop is weak. Last Friday, all three major U.S. stock indexes closed lower, with the Dow down 0.34%, the Nasdaq down 2.15% and the S&#x26;P 500 down 1.09%; the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 3.83%, its eighth straight session of declines, and the memory concept index plunged 6.28%. Japanese and South Korean markets opened lower today, with South Korea's KOSPI down 3.17%, SK Hynix down more than 4% and Samsung Electronics down more than 3%; Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 0.77%. Vishal Khanduja, head of fixed income at Morgan Stanley Investment Management, said at the Global Wealth Management Forum 2026 Shanghai Suhewan Conference that global investment in AI data centers is expected to reach about $5 trillion by 2030, but at least 50% of industry participants may fail to generate returns above their cost of capital over the next five years.
On impact, the policy framing of computing power networks and "AI+" echoes the earnings being delivered in concentrated fashion by supply chain companies, which could in the near term reinforce expectations that the upcycle in AI hardware will continue. However, the sustained correction in overseas semiconductor shares and sharp opening declines in Japanese and South Korean memory stocks may weigh on opening sentiment for related China A-share names. If the technology export catalogue revisions take effect, they will directly affect cross-border technology cooperation models in artificial intelligence, biopharmaceuticals and solar power, and companies involved in outbound licensing and open-source routes will need to reassess compliance arrangements. Buyback proposals from several technology leaders alongside their earnings disclosures are also seen as management's statement on valuation and long-term prospects.
