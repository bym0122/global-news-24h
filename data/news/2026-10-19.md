Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited (NYSE: TSM ) manufactures customers' designs. They are alternative ways to own AI-computing growth, not direct substitutes in the supply chain.
Nvidia vs. TSMC: Which Business Depends Less on Picking the Winning AI Chip? Courtesy of NVIDIA
TSMC's advantage is conditional neutrality: it can earn manufacturing revenue from competing customers when their designs use its processes. A shift toward custom accelerators could therefore leave manufacturing demand intact even if it pressures a merchant-chip supplier. The inference depends on where the replacement chips are made; it is not proof that TSMC captures every design win.
Its third-quarter results reported 79% of wafer revenue from technologies at seven nanometers and below. Two-nanometer products contributed 8%, demonstrating an ongoing process transition. Maintaining that position requires yields, reliable delivery and expensive capacity. Our TSMC-ASML expansion comparison explains how much cash ownership of the factories can consume.
Nvidia's opposing strength is that customers buy usable computing, not merely transistor capacity. The risk is that customers develop enough software and custom hardware to reduce what they pay for that integration.
Diversification changes the risk rather than removing it
