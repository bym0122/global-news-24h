That's a major size advantage that holds its clients captive, because they couldn't move all of their work to another foundry if they tried, since its competitors wouldn't have the necessary scale.
While this advantage could be challenged over time by a rising competitor, there's nobody even close right now. TSMC has great relationships with its clientele, so I doubt its grip on this industry will be broken anytime over the next decade.
While I would say ASML's monopoly status is stronger than TSMC's, I can't say the same about their businesses or stocks.
ASML's business model requires its clients to expand production capacity to sell more machines. While it makes revenue servicing these machines every year, it's not nearly as much as outright selling them.
Business is good for ASML right now because every chip manufacturer (including TSMC) is racing to expand production capacity to meet demand from the AI build-out . However, once the AI build-out is complete, there may not be a need to expand chip production capacity for some time, which could harm ASML's business.
At the same time, while TSMC may not be growing, there could be other technologies that require a substantial number of chips, such as humanoid robots, physical AI, or self-driving cars. That would allow Taiwan Semiconductor to maintain the business size it has achieved, making it a better long-term investment. However, none of this is reflected in the stock's valuation.
ASML trades at a sizable premium to Taiwan Semiconductor when using 2026 earnings estimates. That same trend persists when 2027 earnings are used.
So, ASML is a more expensive stock but with a less certain long-term demand outlook. While monopoly stocks may be safer, I'm more concerned about how they will fare when the current demand wave wraps up. As a result, I think Taiwan Semiconductor is a far safer investment option.

The European Union will shun crude oil and natural gas produced from fields in the Arctic, recommending that these “stay in the ground” under its new Arctic strategy released this week. The reiteration of the EU’s stance on Arctic hydrocarbons comes amid a severe fuel crisis and soaring gas prices ahead of winter peak demand season.
