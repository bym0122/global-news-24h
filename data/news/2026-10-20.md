Probably not. Still, some risks are too loud to tune out, and today's are loud.
We count at least three that threaten the whole market. Each one fits in a single word.
Actually, two wars, fought side by side. One is economic and uses tariffs. The Supreme Court threw out many of them, yet the steel tariffs are still in place, and so are the replacement tariffs. The other uses weapons. It's being fought in Iran and throughout the Middle East, and this month Iran escalated its attacks near the Strait of Hormuz. Then there's Russia-Ukraine, where, as Exxon's chief executive Darren Woods put it in July, Ukraine has disabled about 1 million barrels a day of refining capacity in Russia.
Either kind discourages trade and breaks supply chains. The armed kind also shows up in oil. Crude averaged $60 a barrel in January, passed $90 on March 6 and is above $100 now. On its own, each of the three could be handled. The problem is that all three lean the same way.
One more thing is feeding the fire, and hardly anyone files it under inflation.
More than anything, AI is generating ideas, and a lot of those ideas involve spending. On power and energy. On data centers, equipment, silicon, batteries and building materials. And on energy again, because it bears saying twice: energy goes into everything.
