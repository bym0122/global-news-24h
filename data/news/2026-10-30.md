Investors have had plenty to celebrate over the past four years. The S&P 500 ( ^GSPC -1.49% ) has gained nearly 115% since the fall of 2022, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average ( ^DJI -0.70% ) has gained nearly 80%, and the Nasdaq Composite ( ^IXIC -2.43% ) has jumped more than 150%.
And while it&#x27;s fallen somewhat in the past two weeks, it was just earlier this month that the tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 notched another all-time high.
But as welcome as those gains are, they can also make investors uneasy. It&#x27;s natural. Many investors are wondering if a crash is incoming. So, now is as good a time as ever to consider sage advice from Warren Buffett, one of the most successful investors of all time.
First, while it might feel otherwise, the stock market recording all-time highs isn&#x27;t, by itself, a reason to expect trouble. It&#x27;s all too common for success to make people uneasy.
But consider this: The investment firm Dimensional Fund Advisors looked at S&P 500 data from 1926 through 2022 and found that a year after setting a record, the index was higher 81% of the time and gained an average of nearly 14%.
Of course, crashes still happen. They are a fact of life in the stock market. So, what can we learn from the Oracle of Omaha when the next one happens?
One of Buffett&#x27;s most famous quotes dealt with watching for herd behavior in the market and running in the opposite direction. Since we&#x27;re talking about benefiting from crashing prices, we&#x27;ll stick to the second half of the quote: "Be greedy when others are fearful."
These six words can help you avoid the worst thing you can do during a crash: panic sell. Instead, his advice can help you see a crash as an opportunity: a chance to buy shares of great businesses at steep discounts. For those who can keep their heads, a stock market crash can be lucrative.
A perfect example of this advice in action: the great financial crisis. While most investors were running scared, Buffett poured billions into several companies when confidence was in short supply. This wasn&#x27;t charity: He helped the companies raise cash when they needed it most while ensuring Berkshire Hathaway shareholders made money -- a lot of it.
Here&#x27;s a rundown of his work in 2008 and the years that followed:
Goldman Sachs: Berkshire invested $5 billion in September 2008, receiving preferred shares paying a 10% dividend and warrants -- the right to buy shares at a set price. The deal generated $3.7 billion in profit.
Bank of America: Berkshire invested $5 billion in 2011 as the bank continued to struggle after the crisis. Again, Buffett secured preferred shares and warrants. When he exercised those warrants in 2017, Berkshire had an on-paper gain of $12 billion.
