US stocks fell at the open on Thursday as Alphabet's Q3 earnings sparked a sell-off in the stock and AI jitters returned to markets, dragging on the tech-heavy indexes.
The Dow Jones Industrial Average ( ^DJI ) fell 0.4%, while the S&P 500 ( ^GSPC ) lost 0.3%. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite ( ^IXIC ) fell 1.2%.
Tech stocks snapped a positive run this week as investors digested the Fed's rate pause on Wednesday and earnings from Microsoft ( MSFT ) and Alphabet ( GOOG , GOOGL ). Microsoft beat analyst expectations on the top and bottom lines, while Alphabet sales came up short . Although Google posted cloud growth of 103%, concerns about its free cash flow and AI return on investment were the albatross on the company's quarter.
A sell-off in AI names spread, as the iShares Semiconductor Index ETF ( SOXX ) skidded and software stocks ( IGV ) also tumbled, with double-digit losses for Fortinet ( FTNT ) and ServiceNow ( NOW ) following their earnings reports.
Markets will get another dose of hyperscaler earnings after the bell on Thursday when Amazon ( AMZN ) reports results. Several other stocks were moving in premarket trading on individual earnings: Eli Lilly ( LLY ), for example, popped 4% following strong Q3 results for its GLP-1 drugs. Caterpillar's ( CAT ) stock fell as much as 12% after the industrial giant and key beneficiary of the data center boom forecast weaker-than-expected sales growth. Shares of grocery chain Sprouts ( SFM ) plunged 20% in the wake of disclosing a price cutting strategy expected to weigh on profits into next year.
Thursday's market action comes after the Federal Reserve held rates unchanged on Wednesday afternoon. The decision had two dissenters who favored a rate hike amid sticky inflation. Meanwhile, a fresh reading of the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge showed prices heated up in September, with the Personal Consumption Expenditures index rising 3.6% year over year.
In other news, European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde said on Thursday she will leave her post on Feb. 7, 2027, months ahead of schedule. The early departure comes at a delicate time for Europe, which continues to battle persistent inflation, surging bond yields, and domestic unrest in France over ballooning deficits and proposed austerity measures.
Caterpillar stock drops as AI boom jitters resurface
Caterpillar ( CAT ) shares plunged as much as 12% before trimming losses after the heavy equipment maker's third quarter results only modestly exceeded Wall Street expectations, with adjusted earnings of $7.20 a share and revenue of $20.68 billion.
The industrial giant also forecast moderate sales growth in the fourth quarter, disappointing investors who had expected stronger results from a key beneficiary of the AI infrastructure boom.
The company's power and energy unit, which builds generators and gas turbines for AI data centers, has surged amid the AI boom as the segment's sales have surpassed the big yellow excavators and farming equipment for which it is best known.
Catapillar's order backlog reached a record $78 billion. However, concerns over rising data center costs, project delays, and growing political opposition to new facilities have weighed on sentiment.
Sprout's stock drops 18% as grocery chain embarks on price slashing strategy
Shares of Sprout's Farmers Market ( SFM ) plunged 18% Thursday after the grocery chain cut its profit outlook and announced aggressive price cuts in a bid to win back shoppers.
The Phoenix, Ariz.-based specialty supermarket is embarking on a price-slashing strategy that's now expected to weigh on profits into next year as it's seeing fewer customers.
"We do not expect a smooth road ahead," JPMorgan analyst Thomas Palmer wrote to clients Thursday, describing the earnings reduction as "massive" but keeping its "overweight" rating on the grocery chain.
After warning about third quarter earnings, Sprouts told analysts on Tuesday it expects Q4 earnings of between $0.47 and $0.51 per share, well below the $1.21 analysts had anticipated, according to S&P Global data.
The company cited increasingly price-sensitive customers, along with this year's outbreak of the foodborne parasite cyclospora, as key headwinds driving the sales decline. It's also in the midst of a CEO transition after Sprouts announced last month that COO Nicholas Konat will take over for current CEO Jack Sinclair in early January.
PCE inflation shows prices ticked higher in September, keeping expectations for a year-end hike
A fresh reading of the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge showed prices heated up in September, solidifying expectations for an interest rate hike in December.
The Personal Consumption Expenditures index rose 3.6%, in line with expectations and up from 3.4% a month earlier. On a core basis, which strips out volatile food and energy prices, PCE rose 3.1%, also in line with expectations and up a tenth of a percentage point from 3% in August. Month over month, core PCE clocked in at 0.3%, in line with projections, marking a faster pace from the 0.2% seen in August.
The data was anticipated, prompting many analysts to feel a rate hike was in order Wednesday, given other inflation readings ahead of the Fed's meeting that showed price increases last month.
The US economy grew at a robust pace in the third quarter, driven by investment, consumer spending, and government spending.
Gross domestic product increased at a 3.4% annualized rat e, according to an initial estimate released Thursday by the US Bureau of Economic Analysis, beating economists' expectations of 3.1% and marking an acceleration from the second quarter's 2.2% growth.
Third-quarter growth was partly offset by a decrease in exports. Imports, which are subtracted in the calculation of GDP, increased. The artificial intelligence build-out was the main engine, with business investment surging 12.7%. Consumer spending grew 3.8%, matching the rate in Q2 and the strongest since the end of 2024.
Alphabet heads for a $500 billion drop in market value, the biggest in its history
Alphabet ( GOOGL ) is on track for its worst day ever. The stock fell about 13% Thursday after its third quarter results , steeper than any session since it went public in 2004.
That puts nearly $500 billion of stock market value on the line, the second-biggest one-day loss ever for a US-listed company. Only Nvidia ( NVDA ) has lost more, about $593 billion on Jan. 27, 2025, the day China's DeepSeek rattled AI investors.
Alphabet would need to close at about $274.75, down roughly 15%, to take Nvidia's record, by Yahoo Finance calculations. It has already blown past the $400 billion SpaceX ( SPCX ) lost on June 22, the previous No. 2.

DoorDash acquires Instacart for $17 billion, doubling down on hyper-local delivery
DoorDash ( DASH ) announced plans to acquire hyper-local grocery delivery provider Instacart ( CART ) on Thursday morning in a deal valued at $17 billion.
DoorDash, which is primarily known for its restaurant food delivery business, will pay $65 per Instacart share, a 26% premium over the stock's Oct. 22 closing price. The transaction includes 75% cash, with Instacart stockholders able to elect cash, DoorDash stock, or a combination of both. Instacart's board of directors unanimously approved the deal, according to a press release.
"Grocery is one of the hardest things to get right online," DoorDash CEO Tony Xu said in the release. "Instacart has spent years learning to do that well for grocers, while DoorDash has built a delivery network and technology that local stores rely on every day. Together, we could give stores of every size more of those tools and unlock the potential of grocery delivery."
DoorDash stock popped over 5% in premarket trading, while Instacart stock edged lower.
Lumentum stock jumps on report of possible merger with Marvell
Marvell Technology ( MRVL ) stock dropped 11% on Thursday on reports that the AI chipmaker is in advanced talks to purchase optical parts maker Lumentum ( LITE ). Shares of Lumentum surged 5%.
The potential multibillion-dollar mega deal between the semiconductor designer and Lumentum was first reported by Bloomberg, citing people familiar with the matter.
An agreement could be announced as soon as next week. Lumentum has a market value of about $83 billion, compared with Marvell's roughly $221 billion.
Robinhood is seeing retail investor habits change dramatically as stock trading cools
While overall retail volumes are declining , as Goldman Sachs strategist Ben Snider wrote in a recent note, money is shifting away from traditional equities.
Instead, more investors are pouring their funds into options, leveraged ETFs, and prediction markets. Robinhood's ( HOOD ) latest earnings offer a window into the shift .
The online brokerage reported weaker-than-expected profits earlier this week as declining interest income and rising expenses weighed on the company. But it also drew record trading activity in stocks, options, and prediction markets.
Robinhood said revenue from prediction markets increased more than 500% from this time last year, up to $173 million. Customers traded a record 16.4 billion contracts during the past quarter.
Oil declines as traders parse signs of fresh diplomacy on Iran
Oil dropped as traders weighed fresh diplomatic efforts in the Middle East to avert a potential restart of US military strikes on Iran.
West Texas Intermediate fell toward $102 a barrel, after rising almost 3% in the previous session, while Brent closed near $115. Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said that Tehran continued to give diplomacy chance, and the Wall Street Journal reported US Vice President JD Vance had reached out to Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of the nation's top security body.
President Donald Trump told Axios on Wednesday that negotiations were taking place through mediators but stressed he didn't know whether they would bear fruit. "We need to finish the job — either through a deal or another way — we will find out if negotiations can succeed," he was quoted as saying.

U.S. Natural Gas Production Hits New Records as Demand Surges
U.S. natural gas production is set to hit consecutive records, reaching 112.2 Bcf/d in 2026 and a projected 116.1 Bcf/d in 2027.
Soaring LNG exports and AI data center demand are driving production growth, particularly in the Permian and Haynesville basins.
Producers are consolidating to capitalize on rising demand, highlighted by EQT's acquisition of Range Resources, while stronger output could push Henry Hub prices lower in 2027.
U.S. natural gas production is on track to close a second consecutive year with record-high natural gas output as producers respond to demand signs both at home and overseas.
Soaring demand for powering data centers in the United States and rising liquefied natural gas exports amid expanding liquefaction capacity, as well as surging global demand for spot LNG cargoes in the absence of Qatar’s gas due to the Iran war, are pushing American natural gas production higher this year.
Output is on track to beat in 2026 the record set in 2025 as more associated gas is being produced in the Permian basin along with oil, and operators boost gas output in the Haynesville basin, which is close to LNG export terminals and major domestic industrial gas users, the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) says .
Rising production will more than offset higher LNG exports, resulting in an average Henry Hub spot price for natural gas of $3.16 per million British thermal units (MMBtu) in 2027, down by 9% compared to 2026, the EIA’s latest Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO) showed earlier this month.
For this year, U.S. dry natural gas production is set to jump to 112.2 billion cubic feet per day (bcfd) from 107.64 bcfd last year. In 2027, U.S. natural gas production is forecast to further grow to 116.13 bcfd to another record high, the EIA’s latest monthly estimates showed . In the domestic market, data centers have turned to additional gas-fired power generation to support the booming AI market.
Amid rising gas demand, both in the U.S. and overseas, American gas producers are raising production and entering in strategic transactions to scale and expand.

KYIV, Ukraine — Like many tech entrepreneurs in Ukraine, Yaroslav Azhnyuk turned to developing drone warfare after Russia's 2022 invasion.
Drones have transformed the way wars are fought. Azhnyuk says artificial intelligence is making drones faster and more capable.
"When the other side has orders of magnitudes higher capabilities, it gives you an asymmetric advantage," he says. "It can make you 10,000 times more effective."
Azhnyuk runs the Ukraine-based defense firms The Fourth Law and Odd Systems. They are among the top companies producing AI-powered drones in Ukraine. Dozens of Ukrainian military units already use their autonomous drone, called the Lupynis-10-TFL-1.
Russia is also deploying AI-enabled drones against Ukraine. AI warfare is part of a high-tech arms race that has many worried, including Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. He has warned that AI could lead decisions on the battlefield as soon as next year.
Azhnyuk told NPR that he acknowledges the concern over destructive weapons but says it's not specific to AI.
"It has been with humanity since the invention of fire or steel," he says. "Any kind of tool can be used for good or for bad. And in the end, it's the human that decides how to use that tool."
Azhnyuk is 37, a gregarious computer scientist who hails from a family of scholars and scientists. NPR meets him in a conference room in Kyiv; for security reasons he asked us not to reveal much else about the location.

"These cameras allow pet parents to watch pets when they're not at home," he says, "and fling treats to them and shine a laser toy for them. We sold a million of these devices that are quite popular in the U.S."
After Russia launched its full-scale war, Azhnyuk, by then living in Ukraine, pivoted to the defense space.
"I was like, OK, I've got to move on from Petcube and do something to protect my country," he says. "And so now we're making not cameras that fling treats but cameras that fling explosives at people who came to occupy our land."

"We are leaders in autonomous drones and thermal cameras," he says.

The autonomous Lupynis TFL-1 drone combines a small, affordable FPV drone with an autonomous terminal guidance unit. That means the drone has an onboard computer and AI technology that takes control during the last 500 meters (or 0.3 miles) of the flight.
