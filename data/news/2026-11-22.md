To understand this moment requires rewinding a bit to see how we got here.
In 1974, the U.S. negotiated a deal with Saudi Arabia in which the Gulf country agreed to sell oil in U.S. dollars alone . In return, the U.S. would provide military aid and security. The U.S., then under President Richard Nixon, was looking to secure global demand for the U.S. dollar following the end of the gold standard in 1971. In the wake of the 1973 oil crisis, the U.S. was motivated to solidify its own oil supply chain.
Because oil was and is so fundamental to nearly every industry, the “petrodollar” became ubiquitous, and the dollar became the cornerstone of the global economy: Oil-rich countries needed a place to put their growing reserves of dollars and turned to U.S. Treasuries. Countries buying oil did so in greenbacks.
This cycle has created a currency architecture heavily favoring the U.S. dollar that has persisted for more than 50 years. Saudi Arabia, as well as Qatar, Oman, Bahrain, and the United Arab Emirates, require an estimated $800 billion in supporting reserves as a result of having their currencies pegged to the U.S. dollar. For just the second time ever, Mercury has received a robotic visitor from Earth.
The BepiColombo mission — a joint effort of the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japanese Aerospace Exploration Agency ( JAXA ) — arrived in orbit around Mercury on Saturday (Nov. 21), more than eight years after leaving its home planet.
Before BepiColombo, just one spacecraft had ever circled the solar system's innermost planet: NASA's MESSENGER , which did so from March 2011 to April 2015.
BepiColombo consists of two main spacecraft: ESA's Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) and JAXA's Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (MMO), also known as Mio.
The duo launched — along with a power and propulsion element called the Mercury Transfer Module (MTM) — aboard an Ariane 5 rocket from French Guiana in October 2018 , beginning a long and looping trip through the inner solar system.
Mercury is a difficult planet to reach because it's so close to the sun . Spacecraft approaching the planet are accelerated to high velocities by the immense gravitational pull of our star and must therefore shed a great deal of kinetic energy to be captured by Mercury's much weaker tug.
So the BepiColombo team designed a trajectory that featured one flyby of Earth , two of Venus and six of Mercury over the course of about seven years. That flight time was increased by a year , however, after the mission suffered an issue with its solar panels, which reduced the power available for the MTM's electric thrusters.
BepiColombo's arrival at Mercury will take a total of about six months. (Image credit: European Space Agency) Saturday's orbital insertion came after a 37-minute-long engine burn, which began at 1:24 p.m. EST (1824 GMT). There was a bit drama involved, however, as confirmation of the maneuver's success didn't come for several hours afterward.
"We've heard back from Bepi — the spacecraft is now in contact with the ground and the teams at ESOC [the European Space Operations Centre] are busy assessing the telemetry. Orbital insertion is the latest milestone in BepiColombo's Mercury arrival phase, which is expected to take about six months in total.
Arrival began on Sept. 3, when the MTM released MPO and Mio. The two orbiters remain connected today; they won't separate until Dec. 9 or thereabouts.
Shortly thereafter, Mio will settle into its final orbit, a highly elliptical path that will take it over Mercury's poles. MPO is also destined for a polar orbit, though a much less elliptical one than Mio's; the ESA probe will conduct a series of engine burns before finally getting there on March 10, 2027.
BepiColombo — which is named after the late Italian scientist and mathematician Giuseppe "Bepi" Colombo — won't start doing science work until April 6, 2027. The two probes will gather complementary data from their different vantage points, shedding a great deal of light on a mysterious world.
"The mission will study all aspects of Mercury, from the structure and dynamics of its magnetosphere and how it interacts with the solar wind , to its internal structure with its large iron core, and the origin of the planet’s magnetic field," ESA officials wrote in a BepiColombo mission description .
"The data will enable scientists to understand more about the origin and evolution of a planet located close to its parent star, and a better understanding of the overall evolution of our solar system," they added.
